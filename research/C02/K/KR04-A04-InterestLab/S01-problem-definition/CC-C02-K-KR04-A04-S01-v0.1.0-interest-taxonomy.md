# S01 — Interest and editorial value
SOURCE: Loewenstein (1994), https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75: curiosity as perceived knowledge gap. Silvia (2005), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15755222/: novelty/complexity and coping potential appraisals. Itti & Baldi (2009), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18834898/: surprise and gaze. Berger & Milkman (2012), https://doi.org/10.1509/jmr.10.0353: social transmission, not editorial acceptance. 
PROPOSED: define interest as audience-conditioned potential value of a grounded event given relevant context. Distinguish attention (orienting), curiosity (information gap), novelty (relative to history), understanding, humor, social meaning, emotional significance, information, quotability, narrative contribution, memorability, sharing, replay, and final clip suitability. Evidence may be an unexpected claim, resolved question, contradiction, emotional cause, goal change or visual outcome. Open text reasons, not a mandatory category classifier. Interest and evidence confidence are different; a high-interest hypothesis may need additional evidence. Faithfulness and closure are gates, not interchangeable dimensions. Unusual noise, shocking quotes and repeated reactions are hard negatives. No psychological theory supplies a calibrated scoring function. Audience knowledge and preferences may vary.
